# Brief for independently fact-checking new CPA REG Area II (Business Law) questions

You did not write these questions. Check them as a skeptical reviewer. The bank is the main study source for an accountant preparing for the CPA exam's Regulation (REG) section, so a wrong key teaches a wrong rule.

Scope: REG Area II of the 2026 AICPA blueprint (/home/claude/CPA_Exam_Blueprints_2026.pdf). Its References for business law are the Revised Model Business Corporation Act, the Revised Uniform Partnership Act, the Revised Uniform Limited Partnership Act, the Uniform Commercial Code (UCC), the Bankruptcy Abuse Prevention and Consumer Protection Act of 2005, the Patient Protection and Affordable Care Act, the Foreign Corrupt Practices Act of 1977, and current business law textbooks. The key must be the majority rule: the official UCC text, the federal statute, or the common-law rule most states follow.

For EVERY question in your assigned file:
1. Is the keyed answer ("answer" is a 0-based index into "choices") correct under the majority rule?
2. Is exactly one choice defensibly correct? Flag any question where a careful candidate could defend a second choice, or where the stem leaves out a fact the answer turns on (whether a party is a merchant, a consumer or an insider; whether a writing was signed; how a security interest was perfected; dates).
3. Does it apply the right body of law: common law for services and real estate, UCC Article 2 for goods, Article 9 for security interests, title 11 for bankruptcy?
4. Any factual error in the explanation, including side facts?
5. Is the cited source a reasonable one, and does it actually say what the question relies on? Is "group" (A to E) right?

Verify against: the UCC on law.cornell.edu/ucc; title 11 and title 15 (FCPA, 78dd-1 to 78dd-3 and 78m) on uscode.house.gov or law.cornell.edu/uscode; the IRC and IRS Pub 15 and 15-A (2025) for employment tax and worker status; Cornell Wex (law.cornell.edu/wex) for common-law rules. Do not accept prep-course blogs, law firm marketing pages or Wikipedia. A key that depends on an inflation-adjusted dollar amount (such as 11 U.S.C. 104 thresholds) is a problem.

Do NOT edit any file. Reply with:
- PROBLEMS: each problem question id, what is wrong, the correct rule, the source URL, and a suggested fix (corrected key, reworded stem, or "drop").
- UNVERIFIED: ids you could not verify, with what you tried.
- COUNT: one line with how many were verified OK.
Keep it tight; skip questions that are fine.
