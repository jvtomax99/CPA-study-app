# Brief for independently fact-checking new CPA FAR questions

You did not write these questions. Check them as a skeptical reviewer. The bank is the main study source for an accountant preparing for the CPA exam's Financial Accounting and Reporting (FAR) section, so a wrong key teaches a wrong rule.

Scope: the FAR section of the AICPA's 2026 blueprint (/home/claude/far-blueprint.txt), including its Section assumptions and References. Unless a stem says otherwise, the entity is a for-profit business reporting under U.S. GAAP. The bank keys the Codification as amended by ASUs issued through December 31, 2025.

For EVERY question in your assigned file:
1. Is the keyed answer ("answer" is a 0-based index into "choices") correct? Recompute every calculation, including present values, effective interest schedules, earnings per share denominators, deferred tax balances and consolidation eliminations.
2. Is exactly one choice defensibly correct? Flag any question where a careful candidate could defend a second choice, or where the stem leaves out a fact the answer turns on (entity type, year-end, stated and market rates, useful life, salvage value, tax rate, fair value, an election).
3. Would the key differ under an ASU that is issued but not yet effective for all entities, or under the version it replaced? Flag it.
4. Is it outside the References or the blueprint's limits (IFRS, lessor accounting, government content beyond measurement focus, basis of accounting and fund purpose)? Flag it.
5. Any factual error in the explanation, including side facts and arithmetic?
6. Is the cited source a primary source that says what the question relies on, and is "group" right for the blueprint area?

Verify against the files in /home/claude/far-sources/ (FASB Concepts Statements, ASUs, GASB Statements, AU-C 800) and, for SEC questions, 17 CFR 210, 17 CFR 229 and 15 U.S.C. 78m on law.cornell.edu. Do not accept prep-course blogs, Wikipedia, or a textbook as the only support for a recognition or measurement rule.

Do NOT edit any file. Reply with:
- PROBLEMS: each problem question id, what is wrong, the correct rule or figure, the source (file and paragraph, or URL), and a suggested fix (corrected key, reworded stem, or "drop").
- UNVERIFIED: ids you could not verify, with what you tried.
- COUNT: one line with how many were verified OK.
Keep it tight; skip questions that are fine.
