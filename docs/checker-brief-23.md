# Brief for independently fact-checking new IRS SEE Part 2 and Part 3 questions

You did not write these questions. Check them as a skeptical reviewer. The bank is the main study source for an accountant preparing for the SEE, so a wrong key teaches a wrong rule.

Tax-year rule: the current SEE window (July 1, 2026 to Feb 28, 2027) tests the IRC, regulations, IRS forms, instructions and publications as amended through December 31, 2025, for calendar year 2025. That INCLUDES P.L. 119-21 (signed July 4, 2025), which changed several 2025 figures retroactively. Provisions effective only in 2026 or later must not be the key.

For EVERY question in your assigned file:
1. Is the keyed answer ("answer" is a 0-based index into "choices") correct for 2025 law?
2. Is exactly one choice defensibly correct? Flag any question where a careful candidate could defend a second choice, or where the stem omits a fact needed to answer.
3. Any factual error in the explanation, including side facts and arithmetic? Recompute every calculation.
4. Is the cited source a reasonable primary source?

Verify year-dependent figures (dollar thresholds, percentages, phaseouts, dates) against primary sources with WebSearch/WebFetch: irs.gov publications and 2025 form instructions, Rev. Proc. 2024-40, IRS Notices, IRS newsroom pages on P.L. 119-21, the IRC on law.cornell.edu or uscode.house.gov, eCFR (Circular 230 is 31 CFR Part 10). Do not accept prep-course blogs or Wikipedia. If irs.gov PDFs are blocked, try the HTML versions (irs.gov/publications/pXXX, irs.gov/instructions/iXXXX) or the statute.

Do NOT edit any file. Reply with:
- PROBLEMS: each problem question id, what is wrong, the correct fact, the primary source URL, and a suggested fix (corrected key, reworded stem, or "drop").
- UNVERIFIED: ids you could not verify from a primary source, with what you tried.
- COUNT: one line with how many were verified OK.
Keep it tight; skip questions that are fine.

Extra checks for these parts: a 2025 figure P.L. 119-21 changed (Section 179, bonus depreciation, 1099 thresholds, 163(j), research expensing) must match IRS guidance for 2025, not 2026. A 6695 or other inflation-adjusted penalty dollar amount must not be the key. Circular 230 cites must match the current section numbers.
